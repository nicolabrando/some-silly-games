2D Racing

You qualify before every race. Tick Reverse Grid and the championship is decided by the standings instead — round 1 drawn at random.
How to play

Controls

  • ↑ throttle  ·  ↓ brake (and reverse once stopped)
  • ← → steer  ·  on a phone, use the on-screen pad
  • P or Esc pause — or the Pause button in the corner. Works any time: on the grid, mid‑race, in qualifying. The clock stops with you, so a pause costs you nothing.

Driving the car

The car does not turn on rails. Two things stop it:

  • Understeer. Carry too much speed into a corner and the front washes out: you turn the wheel and the car keeps going straight towards the grass. The cure is to slow down before the corner, not in it.
  • Power oversteer. Squeeze the throttle hard at low speed while turning — a hairpin exit — and the rear steps out and the car spins further than you asked. Ease off, or catch it early.

Both are much easier to provoke in the wet. Black marks on the tarmac mean you are sliding, and sliding is slow.

Surfaces

  • Tarmac — full grip.
  • Kerbs — the red and white strips lining every corner. They cost 20% of your grip and rattle the car, but they are meant to be used: running a wheel over one to straighten a corner is quick. Grass is not.
  • Grass — a third of the grip and heavy drag, and it lines the straights. Running wide there costs you the lap.
  • Barriers — hitting one square costs real damage; brushing along it barely does.
  • Damage. Your car takes less than the AI's, and light contact costs you nothing at all. The AI drives to a computed line with perfect lookahead; you have four arrow keys. A hard enough shunt will still end your race, but one misjudged corner will not.
  • Rain — grip collapses. Corner speeds drop by 10–15%, and some drivers handle it far better than others.

Damage

The bar above each car is its condition. Racing contact is free: leaning on someone, banging wheels at the start, nudging the car ahead into a corner all cost nothing. Past about a third of top speed of closing, damage climbs with the square of the impact, so a real shunt can still end your race. Below 60% the car loses grip and power, at 0% it is out. The drivers race hard and will not simply move over.

Timing tower

Down the left: the order by three-letter driver code (YOU is you), and the gap in seconds to the car directly ahead. Anyone more than a lap behind shows the lap deficit instead — a time gap to a car you have already lapped means nothing.

Championship

A season across every circuit with F1 points. At the end you get the full season grid: every driver, every race, wins and podiums highlighted, DNFs marked, and a ★ against whoever set the fastest lap of each race.

Racing

  • Lights. Move before they go out and you take a 5 second penalty and the grid is reset. The delay before the green is random — do not anticipate.
  • Slipstream. Tuck in behind a car and you get a tow: more power, less drag. It builds the closer you get, so use it down the straight and pull out late.
  • Blue flags. A small blue flag over a car means it is about to be lapped and must let the leader through. If it appears over you, you are a lap down.
  • Qualifying. You qualify before every race. The session is three laps: a warm‑up lap from a standing start, then two flying laps — your best of the two counts. Every opponent runs the same three laps of their own, simulated with the same physics in clean air, and their times drop into the tower on the left as they come in. The classification is the starting grid. Whatever weather qualifying is run in, the race is run in too. You can press End Qualifying to stop early and take whatever you have set. The barriers cost the same as in the race — wreck the car and your session is over on whatever time you had. The car is rebuilt for the race, so you carry the grid slot over, not the damage.
  • Reverse Grid in Championship. Tick it and the championship is decided by the standings instead of by qualifying: the leader starts last, the backmarker starts on pole. The better your season is going, the harder the next race gets. Round 1 has no standings to invert, so that grid is drawn at random.
  • Grand Prix preview. Every championship round opens on a circuit card: a map, length, corner count by hand, an estimated lap time and top speed (measured by actually lapping the circuit), and the weekend's weather. Skip this Grand Prix sits you out: the rest of the field still races the full distance for real championship points, simulated at speed, and you are classified DNS with nothing scored. You still get the full result sheet afterwards.
  • Racecraft. A driver stuck behind someone builds into an attack: they brake later, commit harder and close right up. Once they are alongside they are free to outpace you — so a move that has been started can actually be finished.
  • VSC. If a car is destroyed, a yellow VSC banner appears and every engine is cut to 28% while a crane drives out, lifts the wreck and carries it behind the barriers. Full power returns the moment the track is clear.
  • Grand Chelem. Pole, the win, the fastest lap and leading every single lap of the race — all four in one Grand Prix. It is marked with a gold ★ beside your name in the final championship standings, and on the race itself in the season grid.
  • The finish. Once the leader takes the flag, everyone else is classified on their next crossing of the line. If you are still out there two of the leader's best laps later, you are marked DNF.

The HUD

Lap · position · speed, then the live lap timer: green while you are up on your best lap, red once you have lost it. L is your last lap, B your best, and ★ marks a new personal best.

Opponents

The ten drivers are not reskins of each other. Senna is untouchable in the rain, Verstappen brakes later than anyone and will not yield, Prost never makes a mistake, Vettel is devastating in clean air but fussier in traffic, Alonso is the one you cannot pass. Difficulty scales their pace and their patience; on Impossible they run the true limit of the car and make no mistakes at all.

Free Practice

Your chosen circuit and weather, no opponents, as many laps as you like. Every lap is timed; hit Stop Session in the HUD and you get the full list with the best one marked.

Race Log

Every session is recorded — lap times, flags, contact, retirements, safety car periods, the final classification. Open it from the menu, or download it as a text file.
